*Last updated: 23 October 2026*

Runs reads the running you have already recorded in Apple Health and works out how far next
week can go. It does that on your iPhone and Apple Watch. It has no account, no server, and
nothing to sign in to — unless you connect your own Strava account to push runs there.

## What Runs reads

With your permission, and only with your permission, Runs reads these from Apple Health:

- Workouts — running and walking, indoor and outdoor
- Workout routes, for the runs that have one
- Walking and running distance
- Heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate recovery, and heart rate variability
- VO₂ max
- Active energy
- Body mass

You choose what to grant in Apple's own Health screen, and you can change or withdraw it at
any time in **Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → Runs**. Runs shows whatever it is
given and says so plainly when something is missing.

## What Runs never does

- **It writes to Apple Health only when you merge two workouts.** Settings → Experimental →
  Merge workouts joins a run your watch cut in two into one new workout. Only then does Runs
  ask to write, and it writes nothing but that workout: the two runs' own route and readings
  are linked to it, not copied. Health asks for write access to each kind of reading because
  linking needs it. The two runs are left as they are. Runs never writes anything else, and
  never without you asking.
- **It collects nothing.** Runs has no server and no account. Your workouts, readings and
  routes go nowhere but your own Apple Watch unless you push a run to Strava yourself, under
  Settings → Experimental; then that run's route, heart rate and times go to your own Strava
  account, and nowhere else.
- **No analytics, no advertising, no tracking.** There is no analytics framework in the app,
  no advertising identifier, and nothing that follows you between apps or websites.
- **Nothing is sold or shared.** There is no one to sell it to.

## Where your data stays

On your device. What Runs saves — your unit, which graphs you keep, a ceiling you set by
hand — lives in your app's own storage on the device and is shared only with the Runs widgets
and the Runs watch app. Health data itself is never copied out of Health; it is read when a
screen needs it and shown to you.

Deleting Runs removes what Runs saved, except a Strava connection, which stays in the
iPhone's keychain until you disconnect and forget it in Runs. It does not delete anything in
Apple Health, which is Apple's app and yours to manage.

## Maps

One thing does leave your device: requests for the map under your runs. Runs draws its own
maps — the small map beside a run in the list, the large one at the top of a run, and the
still map you can share — from OpenStreetMap data, which it downloads from
[OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org) for the area a run covers. OpenFreeMap therefore
receives a request for the part of the world your run took place in. It needs no account and
is sent nothing else.

When you download a map for your Apple Watch, in Settings › Watch maps, Runs asks
OpenFreeMap in the same way for a square of about 15 km around where you run most, then
copies it from your iPhone to your own watch, with the routes of your runs across that
square, so the watch can show where you have run. Nothing else is involved.

You can also ask for a map on the watch itself, where its map has none. The watch then asks
your iPhone to download that square, as above; or, with your iPhone out of reach, the watch
downloads it from OpenFreeMap itself, the same way, over its own Wi-Fi or cellular
connection. Either way OpenFreeMap receives requests for that square and nothing else, and
your iPhone keeps a note of the square so the map can be removed from either device.

Three things ask Apple Maps instead: the full-screen map when you switch it to Map or
Satellite, the picture a run is shared with, and the name the watch gives a map you ask for
there — the part of town at its centre. Apple then receives a request for that area, the same
as for any app that shows you a map, and Apple's handling of it is covered by
[Apple's Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).

Map data is kept on your device once downloaded, so scrolling past the same run again asks
for nothing. Your route is never uploaded: it is drawn on top of the map on your device, and
there is no server of ours for it to go to. The only other network use is Strava, and only
for runs you push.

## Location, on the watch

The Runs watch app has a map you can carry offline, and it shows where you are on it. The
first time you open that map, the watch asks to use your location while the app is in use;
it asks for nothing in the background. Your position is read only while the map is on
screen, drawn on it there, and used for nothing else: it is never stored, and never leaves
the watch — not to your iPhone, not to Strava, not to anyone. The one exception is yours to
make: when you tap Download on the watch's map, the middle of the map — which may be where
you are — is where the square to download is centred, and goes, as described under Maps, to
your iPhone, to OpenFreeMap and to Apple Maps. If you say no, the map still
works, without the dot. You can change your mind at any time in the watch's
**Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Runs**. The iPhone app never asks for
your location: its routes come from Apple Health.

## Children

Runs is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone, of any age.
