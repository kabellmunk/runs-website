*Last updated: 17 October 2026*

Runs reads the running you have already recorded in Apple Health and works out how far next
week can go. It does that on your iPhone and Apple Watch. It has no account, no server, and
nothing to sign in to — unless you connect your own Strava account to push runs there.

## What Runs reads

With your permission, and only with your permission, Runs reads these from Apple Health:

- Workouts — running and walking, indoor and outdoor
- Workout routes, for the runs that have one
- Walking and running distance
- Heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate recovery, and heart rate variability
- VO₂ max
- Active energy
- Body mass

You choose what to grant in Apple's own Health screen, and you can change or withdraw it at
any time in **Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → Runs**. Runs shows whatever it is
given and says so plainly when something is missing.

## What Runs never does

- **It writes to Apple Health only when you merge two workouts.** Settings → Experimental →
  Merge workouts joins a run your watch cut in two into one new workout. Only then does Runs
  ask to write, and it writes nothing but that workout: the two runs' own route and readings
  are linked to it, not copied. Health asks for write access to each kind of reading because
  linking needs it. The two runs are left as they are. Runs never writes anything else, and
  never without you asking.
- **It collects nothing.** Runs has no server and no account. Your workouts, readings and
  routes go nowhere unless you push a run to Strava yourself, under Settings → Experimental;
  then that run's route, heart rate and times go to your own Strava account, and nowhere else.
- **No analytics, no advertising, no tracking.** There is no analytics framework in the app,
  no advertising identifier, and nothing that follows you between apps or websites.
- **Nothing is sold or shared.** There is no one to sell it to.

## Where your data stays

On your device. What Runs saves — your unit, which graphs you keep, a ceiling you set by
hand — lives in your app's own storage on the device and is shared only with the Runs widgets
and the Runs watch app. Health data itself is never copied out of Health; it is read when a
screen needs it and shown to you.

Deleting Runs removes what Runs saved, except a Strava connection, which stays in the
iPhone's keychain until you disconnect and forget it in Runs. It does not delete anything in
Apple Health, which is Apple's app and yours to manage.

## Maps

One thing does leave your device. Whenever Runs draws a map — the small map beside a run in
the list, and the large one at the top of a run — it asks Apple Maps for the imagery covering
that area. Apple therefore receives a request for the part of the world your run took place
in, the same as for any app that shows you a map, and Apple's handling of it is covered by
[Apple's Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).

Each map is drawn once and then kept on your device, so scrolling past the same run again
asks for nothing. Your route is never uploaded: it is drawn on top of the map on your device,
and there is no server of ours for it to go to. The only other network use is Strava, and
only for runs you push.

## Children

Runs is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone, of any age.
